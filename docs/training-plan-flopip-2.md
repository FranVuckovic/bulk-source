## How it works, and what to do when it does not

### The week, and which workout is which

**A · Saturday** — lower body A and back. **B · Sunday** — bench volume and shoulders. **C · Monday or Tuesday** — lower body B and the barbell overhead press. **D · Wednesday** — paused bench, upper chest and lats. **E · Friday** — heavy bench.

Thursday is always a rest day, so Friday — the heaviest bench of the week — always follows one. The second rest day is Monday or Tuesday: Lower B goes on whichever of the two you train.

Bench days are B, D and E, and they are never next to each other in any of the three rest-day options. The order is fixed; the days are not. Miss a day and you do the next workout in the order — never two in one day.

### Which rest days, and what changes if you move them

**Monday + Thursday (best):** Fri Sat Sun train, Mon rest, Tue Wed train, Thu rest. Bench Friday, Sunday and Wednesday — 48, 72 and 48 hours apart. The longest run of training days is three.

**Tuesday + Thursday:** Lower B moves to Monday. Exactly the same bench days; the only cost is that Monday is the fourth day in a row.

**Wednesday + Thursday:** Lower B Monday, paused bench Tuesday. Friday gets two rest days in front of it, but Monday and Tuesday are days four and five of a run, so expect those two to be weaker.

What a rest day changes: which session is freshest (the one after it), the gap between sessions that train the same muscles, and how long a run of training days gets. It does not change how much you grow — the same sets get done either way.

### The heavy single: RPE 8 or RPE 9

In volume blocks the Friday single is **RPE 8** — heavy practice at low cost. In heavy blocks it is **RPE 9**: near-maximal practice when it matters most. At a 125 kg max that is about 115 and about 120.

**The 110 kg warm-up decides the day.** If it is fast and clearly has about three reps left, your max is where the app thinks and the target is right. If it feels like an 8, today is a lighter day — take the lighter single.

A 9 means you are certain another rep was there. If it moved like a grinder, it was a 10: log it as a 10.

The back-offs are calculated from the single you log, so the single is the most important number you type all week.

### The AMRAP, every second Friday

One set at a fixed 100 kg, paused, same grip, stopped when another clean paused rep is not there. One extra rep is worth about 3 kg of strength, and you need about 0.6 kg a week to reach the goal — so a rep of progress takes about five weeks. Weekly AMRAPs mostly measure your sleep.

It runs in rotations 2, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 16, 18, 20 and 22: never in a test week, never the week after one, and not in the last two weeks before the final test. In an AMRAP week the back-offs drop from three sets to two.

Extra sets at 100 kg after the AMRAP go in as back-offs, not as more AMRAPs, or the measurement gets mixed with training.

### Failure: which sets, and why not the bench

**To failure (F):** anything on a cable or a machine, and isolation work — laterals, curls, flys, extensions, leg curls, quad extensions, pulldowns, machine rows, leg press. A failed rep there just stops.

**RPE 9 — one rep left, the last set may go to technical failure:** heavy free-weight compounds — dips, weighted pull-ups and chins, dumbbell and incline pressing. On these the form breaks before the muscle fails. The barbell overhead press stays at RPE 8.

**RPE 7–9:** the bench and its variations. Strength does not need failure, fresher bench sessions are heavier bench sessions, and the growth comes from the accessories. The AMRAP is the one bench set to failure, every second week, and it keeps your RPE honest.

Wednesday is the exception: nothing that presses goes to failure, because Friday is 48 hours away.

### Tests, and why there is no weekly max any more

A max test varies about 2–3% from day to day — around 3 kg at 125. Weekly progress is about 0.6 kg. A weekly max mostly measures noise, and a max ladder that adds 2.5 kg on every make writes a good day into every prescription for the next week.

So the plan tests three times — rotations 3, 14 and 25 — each after a week with the same intensity and about half the work. A taper like that raised bench by 2–6% in strength athletes: the strength was there, fatigue was hiding it.

Between tests, the Friday single, the AMRAP and the estimated max from your heavy sets track progress.

### Option A or B, and swapping

Some exercises offer two choices — close-grip or pin press, dips or the Technogym chest press, barbell or dumbbell overhead press. Pick one on the exercise card while you log; the load is worked out for whichever you chose, and History records which it was.

Option A is the default and the one the plan is built around. Option B is there for a reason — pain, equipment, or a heavy block where it suits better.

Anything else can be swapped with **Swap** on the card: search for any exercise by name.

### When to deviate, and how

**You want an RPE 10 single:** Friday only, at most once every four weeks, ideally after a lighter week. Work up in place of the planned single, then skip that day’s AMRAP and do two back-off sets.

**A bad day** (the single is 5% or more below your recent ones): do the single, two back-offs, nothing to failure on compounds. Use the yellow readiness button.

**The Friday single is down two weeks running:** take a light week — half the sets, RPE 7.

**Pain:** up to about 3/10 that does not climb during the session and is gone the next morning — carry on. Anything more: take option B, or swap, for one to two weeks, then try again. Elbow: Monday’s overhead extension becomes a pushdown first, then dips become the chest press. Front of the shoulder: drop the barbell overhead press to dumbbells. Wrist: wraps on pressing, neutral-grip pulldown.

**Missed a day:** do the next workout in the order. After seven or more days off, one week at RPE 7.

### Working maxes

Every load is worked out from a working max: the stable number prescriptions are calculated from. It is not a record. Your bench is your own number — Settings → Working maxes shows every one and lets you change it.

For a lift with no max you have set yourself, the app uses what your recent sets show (the middle of your best estimated maxes over the last six weeks), and says so. Bench variations follow from your competition bench by their ratio until you set their own.

Rule for moving the bench max: if the prescribed weights land a whole RPE off target in two sessions in a row, move it by 2.5 kg.
