# Why the Plan screen is orange — and what to do about it

**Status: DECIDED, 20 August 2026. Option 1 implemented in v2.7.0, and the
Deloads text replaced.** The counting method, the target bands and the plan
itself are still unchanged — option 1 was the one that required no training
change, which is why it was recommended. Options 2 and 3 were not taken; they
are training content and remain open if you ever want them.

What shipped, beyond the option as written: a muscle that is nobody's head —
forearms, quads, hamstrings, calves, glutes — is judged like a roll-up, because
its own figure already is a whole-muscle number; the deltoid heads have no
roll-up and so are not judged at all; and there is a maintenance band between
the published floor of 4 sets and the growth range at 10, so an accessory held
on purpose is not coloured as a shortfall.

The rest of this document is the analysis the decision was made on, kept as
written.

**Written:** 19 August 2026 · against `data/plan-fopip-v2.json`, rotation 12

## The short version

You asked which of three things is true. The answer is **a different one for
each muscle**, which is why it has been hard to see:

| Head | Over by | What is actually causing it |
|---|---|---|
| **Biceps — short head** | 13.4 vs 6–12 | **Counting.** 60% of the total is indirect work at 0.3–0.4 per set. Direct curl work is only 5.0 sets. |
| **Forearms & brachialis** | 12.9 vs 8–12 | **The plan.** 93% direct — six dedicated slots, one per session. Nothing is accumulating; it really is that much. |
| **Delts — side** | 20.9 (rot 12), 24.9 (rot 1, 5, 20) vs 16–22 | **Neither.** 4.0 of it is the myo-rep multiplier, and the rest is six honest lateral-raise slots. |

And one finding you did not ask about, which I think matters more than any of
the above:

> **The per-head bands have no published basis.** The literature gives volume
> landmarks per *muscle*, not per *head*. There is no study that says the biceps
> short head wants 6–12 sets a week. The bands are an extrapolation, and the
> orange is generated by comparing real numbers against them.

The codebase already knows this. `js/volume.js` line 24:

```js
/**
 * The ~20-sets-per-week literature uses whole-muscle units, which is why the
 * roll-ups exist. Only claim diminishing returns when the ROLL-UP is ≥ 20 —
 * never when a single head is.
 */
export const DIMINISHING_RETURNS_SETS = 20;
```

That comment is a rule about not making per-head claims against literature
numbers — and the Plan screen's orange bars are exactly that comparison.

## What the evidence actually supports

### Fractional counting itself is well supported

The largest meta-regression to date (67 studies, 2,058 participants) tested
three ways of counting indirect sets — as 0, as 0.5, and as 1.0 — and found the
**fractional method (0.5) had the strongest relative evidence for hypertrophy,
Bayes Factor 9.48** against the total method. Distinguishing direct from
indirect work is not optional: it materially changes the dose-response fit.

So the plan and the app are right to count fractionally. That part is settled.

### But 0.5 is a fitted coefficient, not a measurement

This is the part that bears on your question. **0.5 is the single value that
best fit the pooled data across 67 studies.** It is not a biological constant,
and no study has measured the indirect contribution of a specific exercise to a
specific muscle. A lat pulldown almost certainly stimulates the biceps at
something other than exactly half; a close-grip bench almost certainly hits the
triceps at more than half.

**The app does not use 0.5.** It uses a graded scale — 1.0, 0.5, 0.4, 0.3, with
anything under 0.3 dropped. Those intermediate values are a refinement of the
published method, not the published method. They are defensible as judgement,
but they have no separate evidence behind them, and they are where the biceps
number comes from.

Worth knowing which direction that cuts:

| Counting rule | Biceps short head |
|---|---|
| As built (graded 0.3–1.0) | **13.4** |
| Strict published method (all indirect = 0.5) | **16.0** |
| Direct + strong indirect only (drop below 0.5) | **7.5** |

Moving to the *actual published method* makes the orange worse, not better. The
app is already more conservative than the literature on those rows.

### Per-head landmarks do not exist

Volume landmarks in the literature — MEV roughly 4–8 weekly sets, MAV roughly
10–20 for trained lifters, diminishing returns for hypertrophy from around 10 —
are all **per muscle**. Regional hypertrophy is real: exercises at long muscle
lengths grow the distal region preferentially, and different exercises bias
different heads. But "this head wants 6–12 sets" is not a claim the evidence
supports, in either direction.

## Where the plan actually sits, in the units the research uses

Whole-muscle roll-ups, counting the largest head per set rather than summing
heads:

| Rotation | Chest | Triceps | Back | Biceps | Core |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 31.2 | 27.7 | 22.0 | 20.9 | 12.0 |
| 12 | 29.2 | 26.7 | 22.0 | 19.9 | 10.0 |
| 20 | 30.2 | 27.2 | 22.0 | 20.9 | 12.0 |
| 30 | 28.2 | 24.7 | 21.2 | 17.9 | 8.0 |

**This is the number worth your attention, and it is not the one the screen is
colouring.** Chest at 28–31 and triceps at 25–28 fractional sets a week sit
above the 10–20 MAV range, in the units the range is stated in. Biceps and back
sit comfortably inside it.

Two honest caveats before you act on that:

- The dose-response meta-regression found hypertrophy **still increasing at the
  top of the volume range studied**, with no clear ceiling. Above 20 is not
  established as wasted — it is established as past where the evidence is
  strong.
- Chest and triceps volume is the *mechanism* of this plan. Four bench
  exposures a week is what buys the frequency effect, and the plan-rationale
  document is explicit that the limit is recovery, joints and session length
  rather than stimulus. High chest and triceps numbers are the plan working as
  designed, not a bug in it.

Also note `plan-rationale.md` §"Per-session volume has a ceiling": no single
session exceeds ~11 fractional sets for one muscle. That constraint is
satisfied. What is high is the weekly total, which is the one the same document
argues has no clear ceiling.

## The decomposition, exercise by exercise

Rotation 12, all six sessions. Speed bench excluded (it counts zero by design).

### Biceps — short head · 13.4 against a 6–12 band

```
  DIRECT      5.0
    F  High-cable / stable machine curl        w=1.0  x3 = 3.0
    C  EZ-bar or preacher curl                 w=1.0  x2 = 2.0
  INDIRECT    7.4   (60% of the total)
    D  Weighted chin-up — supinated            w=0.5  x3 = 1.5
    B  Incline dumbbell curl                   w=0.4  x3 = 1.2
    D  Bayesian cable curl                     w=0.4  x3 = 1.2
    A  Chest-supported / cable row             w=0.3  x3 = 0.9
    C  One-arm lat-biased cable row            w=0.3  x3 = 0.9
    F  Stable machine / chest-supported row    w=0.3  x3 = 0.9
    D  Reverse cable / EZ curl                 w=0.4  x2 = 0.8
```

**Your first hypothesis is correct here.** Three rowing slots contribute 2.7
sets of "biceps short head" at 0.3 each. Two of the four curl variations are
scored 0.4 rather than 1.0 — incline and Bayesian curls are scored as *long*-head
biased, which is right, but it means the short head accumulates most of its
total from work not aimed at it.

### Forearms & brachialis · 12.9 against an 8–12 band

```
  DIRECT     12.0   (93% of the total)
    A  Supported wrist flexion                 w=1.0  x2 = 2.0
    B  Hammer or reverse curl                  w=1.0  x2 = 2.0
    C  Supported wrist extension               w=1.0  x2 = 2.0
    D  Reverse cable / EZ curl                 w=1.0  x2 = 2.0
    E  Cable pronation / supination            w=1.0  x2 = 2.0
    F  Wrist roller / flexion-extension        w=1.0  x2 = 2.0
  INDIRECT    0.9
```

**Nothing is accumulating.** Every session carries a dedicated forearm slot.
The plan prescribes twelve direct sets a week and the app reports twelve. If
12.9 against 8–12 is wrong, the band is wrong or the prescription is — the
counting is not involved.

### Delts — side · 20.9 at rotation 12, 24.9 at rotations 1, 5 and 20

```
  DIRECT     16.0
    A/C/E/F  Cable lateral raise               w=1.0  x3 = 3.0 each
    B/D      Cable lateral raise               w=1.0  x2 = 2.0 each
  INDIRECT    0.9
    F  Overhead press ladder                   w=0.3  x3 = 0.9
  MYO-REP MULTIPLIER  +4.0
```

**A third mechanism you did not list.** A myo-rep set counts as **2**
(`MYO_REP_SET_VALUE`), on the reasoning that a cluster is worth about two
straight sets and counting its mini-sets individually would score it as five.
Four of the six lateral-raise slots are myo-rep, so 4.0 of the 20.9 is that
multiplier. Count a myo-rep as one straight set and side delts read 16.9 —
inside the band.

The multiplier is a defensible judgement and I am not proposing changing it.
But it means the side-delt orange is substantially a *counting-convention*
artefact rather than a statement about how many hard sets of laterals you do.

## What I would propose

Ranked. Each is independent — you can take one and not the others.

### 1. Stop colouring per-head bars against per-head bands · **recommended**

The strongest option, and the only one that changes no training content at all.

Show the per-head numbers, because they are useful and real. **Colour the
whole-muscle roll-up instead**, against the 10–20 range the literature actually
states, and leave the head bars a neutral colour with their band drawn as a
reference marker rather than as a verdict.

- Changes: `js/ui/plan.js` (`volumeBars`), and the legend text.
- Training content changed: **none.** Same sets, same reps, same counting.
- Effect: the orange disappears from the heads, and appears on chest and
  triceps where the whole-muscle figure genuinely sits above the range the
  research supports — which is the honest signal.
- Why I favour it: it is what `DIMINISHING_RETURNS_SETS`' own comment already
  says the app should do, and it stops the screen making a claim the evidence
  cannot support in either direction.

### 2. Widen or remove the specific bands that have no basis

If you want to keep per-head colouring, the three persistent offenders need
bands that mean something. Biceps short head at 6–12 while the whole biceps
sits at 19.9 in a 10–20 range is not a coherent pair of statements.

- Changes: `lo`/`hi` in `data/plan-fopip-v2.json` under `muscles`.
- **This is training content. Your call entirely — I would not pick numbers.**

### 3. Reduce direct forearm work

The only place the plan genuinely prescribes more than its own band allows.
Six dedicated slots, one per session, is a lot of dedicated forearm work for a
bench-focused bulk — and `plan-rationale.md` §5 already lists **elbows** as the
second-highest risk in the plan, naming grip and forearm work among the load on
that joint.

- **This is training content and I have not touched it.** I raise it only
  because it is the one head where the number is not an artefact of anything.

### 4. Leave it exactly as it is

Defensible. Growth was still increasing at the top of the studied volume range;
the per-session ceiling of ~11 is respected everywhere; nothing here is
physically impossible or self-contradictory. "Above the band" is not "wrong",
and the plan's chest and triceps volume is the mechanism it works by.

If you pick this, I would still suggest **option 1**, because it costs no
training change and stops the screen overstating what it knows.

## Separately: one piece of in-app text contradicts the plan

Found while checking that the General tips still describe FOPIP. Not about set
counting, but it is training guidance and therefore yours to decide, so it is
here rather than fixed.

**Plan → Tips → Cardio and life → "Deloads"** currently reads:

> Week 6 of every block. Keep all six sessions, cut volume 40–50%, drop top-end
> intensity about 10%, everything at 3+ RIR minimum. Never take a full rest
> week…

That is a **scheduled** deload, on a **calendar week**. The plan you are
actually running has neither:

- `docs/plan-rationale.md` §"Why deloads are earned rather than scheduled" says
  planned deloads slightly reduce strength gains against training through, and
  that the app watches your own triggers instead.
- `docs/v2-status.md` records the same decision: *"Deloads are not scheduled.
  The kit keeps a Recovery Rotation 15, which is implemented."*
- The app implements exactly that: Recovery Rotation 15, plus a deload offered
  when two of your own triggers fire together.
- And "week 6 of every block" is a v1 sentence in a plan where **blocks advance
  on sessions completed, not on weeks** — the retired structure the whole v2
  rewrite moved away from.

So the app's own advice screen tells you to deload on a schedule the app does
not have, in units the plan does not use. Of everything in this document this
is the one I would fix first, because unlike the orange bars it can be acted on
directly and wrongly.

Suggested replacement, for you to approve or rewrite — the volume and intensity
numbers are carried over unchanged, only the trigger changes:

> **Earned, not scheduled.** There is no fixed deload week. Rotation 15 is a
> planned Recovery rotation, and beyond that the app offers a deload when two of
> your own triggers fire together — the readings it is already watching. Taking
> one: keep all six sessions, cut volume 40–50%, drop top-end intensity about
> 10%, everything at 3+ RIR minimum. Never take a full rest week: complete
> cessation was found worse than training through.

**Done in v2.7.0**, with the suggested text, in `data/plan-fopip-v2.json` under `knowledge`. "When things go wrong" was corrected in the same pass: it said to pull the deload forward, which implied a scheduled one.
