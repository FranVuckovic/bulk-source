## 4. Your plan, with the parts I would change

> *"try to hit 120 kg first and then calibrate after that… increase that
> single by 2.5 kg every week"*

**Open at 115, not 120.** 115 was RPE 9–10 for you twice in August. 120 is
about 104% of your best honest estimate. A missed single with a back that is
already complaining is a bad first move, and the calibration you want comes
from a *made* lift, not a failed one.

Concretely: **work to 115 paused. If it goes at RPE 8 or less, take 117.5.**
If that goes at RPE 8 or less, you have earned 120 and you can take it next
week. That is three possible outcomes and all of them give you a number.

**+2.5 kg a week is not a schedule, it is a wish.** Thirty-three rotations at
+2.5 is +82.5 kg. Replace the calendar with a condition:

> **Add 2.5 kg to the single only when the previous week's single was RPE 8
> or below.** Otherwise repeat it.

That is the same intent, it cannot outrun you, and it self-corrects on a bad
week. Early on you will add nearly every week; later you will not, and that
is the information.

**Heavy single then AMRAP in the same session — yes, but know the cost.** The
single will take something off the AMRAP. That is acceptable, but it makes
the new AMRAPs **not comparable with your August ones**. Start a fresh series
from the day you change it and do not read the step as a loss of strength.

**AMRAP at a fixed 100 kg — good, and better than the current design.** A
fixed load with a rep count is the cleanest measurement there is. At a 115 max
it is 87%, the top of the useful window, which is fine. Two rules make it
work:

1. **Every rep paused, one second, no exceptions.** If the pause breaks, the
   set stops. A rep you bounced is not part of the count.
2. **Same load until your max moves**, then move it in one step and start a
   new series.

**Rest day before C — yes.** I argued this before: C is the only measurement
in the plan, and every other session is autoregulated by RPE so turning up
tired costs you almost nothing there. C is the one place tired means *wrong*,
not just *worse*.

## 5. Programming the variations

You have `benchVar` tracked as its own working max already, which is the right
structure. What is missing is which variation, and at what fraction.

### Typical fractions of a paused competition 1RM

These are population averages. Yours will differ, and the point of tracking
each separately is to find out by how much.

| Variation | % of comp 1RM | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Competition paused | 100% | The reference |
| Touch-and-go | 102–105% | Not a training tool. This is where your inflation came from |
| **Long pause, 3–5 s** | **85–92%** | Off-the-chest strength, and it kills the bounce |
| **Spoto (stop 2–3 cm up)** | **85–90%** | Tension and control; no chest contact at all |
| **Pin press at chest height** | **85–92%** | The purest off-the-chest strength — zero elastic contribution |
| **Close/narrow grip** | **88–93%** | Triceps and lockout |
| **Wide grip** | **95–100%** | Shorter range, harder lockout |
| Pin press, mid-range | 95–105% | Lockout only |
| Feet-up / Larsen | 90–95% | Removes leg drive; upper-body-only strength |
| Incline 30° | 75–82% | Upper chest, different angle |

### How to program them

**One variation per block, not one per week.** A variation you meet once is a
novelty; you cannot progress what you do not repeat. Blocks are 8 rotations —
that is 8 exposures, enough to add weight to it several times.

**Given what your data says about you** — strong lockout relative to a weak,
bounce-dependent bottom — the order I would run them:

| Block | Variation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Now** | **Long pause, 3 s** | Directly attacks the thing the AMRAP exposed |
| Next | Pin press at chest height | Same weak point, zero elasticity, heavier absolute loads |
| Then | Close grip | Once the bottom is fixed, build the lockout |
| Later | Wide grip or feet-up | Variety, and a different check on the same lift |

**Track each with its own working max.** The app already keeps `benchVar`
separate from `benchComp` — the missing piece is that the *variation* changes
and the max should change with it. That is an app change and it is listed
below.

## 6. The form-breakdown flag — yes, and here is the shape

Your set records already carry `pauseStyle`, `gripWidth` and `variantUsed`.
Adding one more field is cheap and it is the right fix:

**`formBreakdown: true`** on a set means:

- the set is **kept exactly as logged** — it happened, it counts as volume,
  it appears in the Log;
- it is **excluded from records** and from any working-max proposal;
- the exclusion is **reported with its reason**, the way every other exclusion
  in this app is, so the Progress screen says "1 set excluded — form
  breakdown" rather than silently dropping it.

Two more changes I would make alongside it:

1. **The AMRAP asks for pause style before it lets you log it.** A blank
   `pauseStyle` on the set that defines your training is the specific hole
   that let this happen. Both your AMRAPs have it blank.
2. **A quiet warning when a logged load is far above the prescription.** Not a
   block — you are allowed to go heavier — but "prescribed 97.5 at RPE 7, you
   logged 105 at RPE 10" is worth seeing at the moment you log it, not four
   sessions later.
