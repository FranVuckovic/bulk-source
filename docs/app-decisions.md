# Part 0 · Answers to your three direct questions

## What Session RPE means

A single 0–10 rating of **how hard the whole session felt**, given about 20 minutes after you finish — not during, because during a session you always feel worse than you are.

Multiplied by session duration it produces a **session load** (Foster's method, a validated measure): a 75-minute session at sRPE 7 = 525 units. Sum those over seven days and you have weekly load.

**Why bother:** weekly load usually climbs for a week or two *before* you consciously feel run down. It is the earliest available warning that fatigue is accumulating, and it costs one tap. It is a trend instrument, not a per-session one — a single sRPE 8 means nothing; three weeks of rising load while your top sets flatten means pull the deload forward.

## How maxes get tracked and updated — the full protocol

**Which lifts have a stored max (7):** competition bench, bench variation, incline press, weighted pull-up, weighted dip, leg press, overhead ladder. Nothing else. Estimated maxes on isolation work are not meaningful — one validation study found *all nine* standard prediction equations failed on the arm curl.

**Two separate numbers per lift:**

| | What it is | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Observed e1RM** | Computed from every index set. A continuous history. Never overwritten. | Every session |
| **Working max** | The number prescriptions are calculated from. | **Block boundaries only** |

**Where observed e1RM comes from — index sets only:**

| Lift | Index set | To failure? |
|---|---|---|
| Comp bench | AMRAP in E (first exercise) **and** top single in A — take the higher | AMRAP yes |
| Incline press | First working set, C | No, RPE 8 |
| Weighted pull-up | First working set, A | No, RPE 8 |
| Weighted dip | First working set, E | No, RPE 8 |
| Leg press | First working set, B | No, RPE 8 |
| Bench variation | First working set, E | No |
| Overhead ladder | First working set, D | No |

Sets above 12 reps produce no e1RM — the estimate stops being meaningful there.

**The update rules:**

1. **At each block boundary** (every 6 weeks) the app proposes: *working max = best observed e1RM from that block's index sets*.
2. **You confirm with one tap.** Never silent, never automatic.
3. **Mid-block early bump** only if observed exceeds working by **more than 5% for two consecutive weeks**.
4. **Never lowers mid-block.** A bad week is a bad week, not a strength loss.
5. **At a block boundary it can lower**, but by a maximum of 5% at a time, and it says so loudly.
6. **Deload weeks never trigger an update** — loads are deliberately low.
7. **Manual override always available**, logged with date.

**Why the working max is frozen between blocks — this is the part that matters.** Test–retest variability on a 1RM in trained lifters is about 3.3%, which on a 130 kg bench is ±4 kg of pure noise. If the working max ratcheted up every good session, six weeks later you would be training at a genuine RPE 9.5 while believing you were at 8. That is overreaching by arithmetic, and it is essentially undetectable from the inside. The frozen working max is the mechanism that stops your old failure mode returning through the back door.

Isolation sets with 60–90 s rests cost almost nothing systemically. The real constraint was session length, not recovery — which is why the fly went into F, the shortest session with no pressing.

**A2 · Should we push the aesthetic volume further?** — my recommendation: **not with straight sets**

You are now at 13 upper chest, 21 triceps, 12.6 biceps long head. Going higher with more sets has a poor return: past roughly 20 sets per muscle per week the curve flattens hard — going from 15 to 30 sets might yield only ~20% more growth, not 100%. And every extra set costs minutes in sessions that are already 65–85 minutes.

**The better lever is intensity technique, not more sets.**

**A3 · Add myo-reps to three designated isolation exercises?** — 🟡 recommended

A myo-rep set is: one activation set to failure at 12–20 reps, rest 15–20 s, then 3–5 mini-sets of 3–5 reps with 15–20 s between. Total time about two minutes, and it delivers roughly the stimulus of three straight sets.

Proposed on: **cable lateral raise** (one session), **overhead triceps extension**, **incline/Bayesian curl**. That is roughly +6 effective sets per week across your three priority muscles for about +4 minutes total.

Cost: the app needs a myo-rep set type (moderate complexity), and it is genuinely unpleasant. Say no if you would rather keep it simple.

# Part B · New visualizations

Ranked by value per unit of work.

**B1 · Consistency heatmap** — 🟢 · fragility low · ✅ **strongly recommended**
A calendar grid, one cell per day, coloured by which session you did. **Your single biggest risk factor is not finishing 33 weeks**, and nothing else in the app makes adherence visible. Cheap to build, and it is the chart you will look at when motivation dips.

**B2 · Load–rep scatter with iso-e1RM curves** — 🟡 · low · ✅ **recommended**
Every bench set ever, plotted as load against reps, with curved lines marking equal estimated maxes. Instead of one number moving, you see the whole cloud of your capability shifting up and right. It also shows *where* you are improving — a rising cloud on the low-rep side but not the high-rep side means your strength is improving faster than your work capacity, which is diagnostic.

**B3 · Strength vs bodyweight, indexed** — 🟢 · low · ✅ **recommended**
Both lines indexed to 100 at week 1, on one chart with one axis. Answers the question that actually matters on a bulk: *is my strength outpacing my mass, or am I just getting heavier?* If the strength line pulls away, the bulk is working.

**B4 · Weekly session load** — 🟢 · low · ✅ **recommended**
Sum of sRPE × duration per week, with a rolling average. The early-warning instrument described above. Only works if you log sRPE.

**B5 · Volume by muscle over time** — 🟡 · low · recommended
Are you actually accumulating what the plan says? Catches quiet drift — the accessories you have been skipping for a month without noticing.

**B6 · PR timeline** — 🟢 · low · recommended
Every PR as a dot on a time axis, coloured by lift. Motivating, and it shows whether PRs are clustering (good block) or drying up (time to change something).

**B7 · Block comparison** — 🟡 · low · recommended
A bar per block showing e1RM gained. After three blocks you can see which structures actually worked *for you*, which is worth more than any general recommendation I can give.

**B8 · Estimated lean vs fat mass** — 🟡 · medium · **skip for now**
Derivable from weight plus waist. Directionally interesting, but the error bars are wide enough that it may mislead you more than the waist number alone. The waist trend already answers this.

**B9 · Per-exercise sparklines** in the Exercises section — 🟢 · low · optional
A tiny inline chart on each exercise. Nice, not important.

Tap the bench, get today's ramp already worked out: empty bar → 40 → 60 → 80 → 92 → 100 → work set. Scales with the day's top set. Used every single session, takes an hour to build.

# Part D · Extra things worth tracking

Each of these is one small field. The test is whether it changes a decision.

**D1 · Grip width used on bench** — ✅ recommended. You are about to test grip widths; without recording it you cannot tell which one produced which result.

**D2 · Incline variant (DB or Smith)** — ✅ recommended. They rotate by block, and 44 kg of dumbbell is not 44 kg on a Smith. Without this field the incline progression is uninterpretable.

**D3 · Pause length on bench (paused / touch-and-go)** — ✅ recommended. A touch-and-go rep is worth about 4% more than a one-second pause. Mixing them silently corrupts your e1RM trend. This is the single most important comparability field.

**D4 · Which grip on pull-ups** — ✅ recommended. Same reason.

**D5 · Session duration** — ✅ free, already captured from timestamps. Also the instrument that catches you rushing again.

**D6 · Time of day** — ✅ free from the timestamp. Performance varies by several percent across the day; worth having when a session looks anomalous.

**D7 · Caffeine / pre-workout, yes-no** — 🟢 recommended. Pre-workout is worth a meaningful fraction of a rep on an AMRAP. If you take it on some measurement days and not others, your progress line has noise in it that looks like real change.

**D8 · Stress or mood, 1–5** — 🟢 optional. Cheap, and it correlates with readiness better than most things. Say no if it feels like admin.

**D9 · Daily steps** — 🟢 optional. You walk a lot and it is a real part of your energy balance. Manual entry, one number.

**D10 · Bar speed** — field already exists, unused unless you get a VBT app.
