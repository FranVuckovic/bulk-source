## How to run it

The rotation is **D · E · F · C · B · A**, and it repeats. It is a training unit, not a calendar week — you always just do the next one.

The recommended week, which is how it is actually run:

| day | session | |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **D** | Lower body, back, biceps, rear/side delts and core |
| Tue | **E** | Bench volume, incline Smith and chest/triceps |
| Wed | **F** | Speed bench, overhead work, back and delts |
| Thu | **rest** | The rest day goes immediately before C — C is the measurement session and a tired reading is a corrupted reading. |
| Fri | **C** | Weekly attempt, 100 kg AMRAP, bench variation, chest and triceps |
| Sat | **B** | Lower body, glutes, biceps, core and side delts |
| Sun | **A** | Heavy bench, upper chest, back and triceps |

Four pressing days in seven means at least one adjacent pair, always. This order leaves exactly one, E → F, in that direction: the speed work is the most fatigue-sensitive thing in the plan so it takes the tired slot, and E's volume is RPE-autoregulated so it degrades gracefully. A gets two clear days behind it, and C gets the rest day.

## The bench: five lifts, five numbers

Every variation has its own 1RM, stated as a fraction of the competition max. The app multiplies to get the variation's max, then applies the RPE table to get the working weight. Confirm a real max for a lift and the ratio stops being used.

| lift | ratio | band | its max | where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Competition bench press | **the anchor** | — | 120.0 kg | A2, A3, C11, C1, E1 |
| Speed bench | n/a — a stated % of the competition max | — | — | F1 |
| Close-grip bench press | 93% | 90–96% | 112.5 kg | F8 |
| Long-pause bench press — 2–3 s | 94% | 90–97% | 112.5 kg | E2 |
| Pin press — bar set just below the sticking point | 90% | 85–97% | 107.5 kg | C2 |
| Wide-grip bench press | 93% | 90–97% | 112.5 kg | *substitute only* |
| Feet-up bench press | 92% | 90–94% | 110.0 kg | *substitute only* |

**The competition bench is always controlled touch-and-go.** All paused competition-grip work lives in the long-pause lift, so the two keep separate numbers and neither pollutes the other.

**The attempt** opens at 120.0 kg and adds 2.5 kg after every one you make. A miss repeats the same weight. Made writes it as the new working max, and everything above recalculates from it.

**The AMRAP** is a fixed 100 kg, paused style logged, so it measures reps at a constant load rather than estimating a max the attempt already measures directly.

## Where every number comes from

- **Working load** = the lift's own max × the RPE table's percentage for that slot's reps and RPE. Nothing is a fixed percentage of anything except the AMRAP and the speed work.
- **Speed work** is a stated percentage of the competition max (64% in Accumulation I), not an RPE lookup, because the point is bar speed rather than effort.
- **The AMRAP** is a fixed load. It used to be 83% of the working max; it is a constant weight now so the rep count is the measurement.
- **Back-offs** are taken from the day's actual top single and the target RPE, not from a fixed share of the working max.
- **Recalibrating a ratio**: set the weight from the percentage, log the RPE as information, and after three or four exposures move the ratio 0.02 if the RPE is consistently a point high or low. Sanity-check the *working weight*, never the derived max — a small ratio error is multiplied twice before it reaches the bar.
- **The fastest way out of guessing** is to confirm a real max for each variation. One heavy set of each and the ratios stop being used at all.
