### Exactly what changed, and why each one

**D2 · the chin-up moves to second.** No change to the exercise, the sets, the
reps or the RPE. It is the best thing in the session and you were doing it
fifth, after two leg exercises. Order costs nothing and changes how a session
feels more than most people expect. The jumps stay first because they are
explosive work that needs you fresh, and they are three cheap sets at RPE 6.

**D3 · single-leg leg press replaces the hack squat / Bulgarian slot.** You have
no hack squat, Bulgarians hurt your back, and the leg press loads light enough
to run one leg at a time. Unilateral pressing is a genuinely different exercise,
not a scaled one: more range at the hip, nowhere for the strong side to hide,
and half the systemic cost. Your quads measure 55.6 against 56.2 cm, so there is
something to find. Reps go 8–12 → 10–15 per leg because unilateral work is
steadier at slightly higher reps.

**D4 · hip thrust replaces the second leg press.** This is the change with the
largest effect in the whole table, and I would argue for it even if nothing else
moved. **Your glutes get 3.0 sets a week, in one session out of six** — by a
distance the worst-served muscle in the plan, against a productive band of
10–20. You have a hip thrust *machine*, so it is quick to set up and cheap to
run twice a week. A second leg press in the same session was the least useful
slot in D anyway.

**D5 · 45° back extension replaces the lying leg curl.** Not a like-for-like
swap, and deliberately so. The hamstrings do two separate jobs — bending the
knee and extending the hip — and the plan only trained the first. Session B's
seated curl keeps the knee-flexion work; this adds the hip-extension work that
was missing entirely. It also loads the glutes at a long length, where the hip
thrust loads them short, which is why the pair is worth more than either twice.
For your back: stop the top of the rep when your torso is **in line** with your
legs, never above it.

**D8 · hanging leg raise replaces the ab wheel.** You have never done a rollout
and are unsure of the form, and it is the exercise most likely to teach a bad
habit under load — with a back that already objects. You have done leg raises
before. Same slot, same sets, same job.

**D9 · standing calf raise, 3 sets.** You have no seated calf machine, so the
seated slot was never happening. This is where one of the two removed sets comes
from. The cost is honest: standing raises train the gastrocnemius, and the
soleus needs a bent knee, so **the soleus stays untrained** unless you improvise
one (bench, plate across the knees, ball of the foot on a block).

**Removed · reverse hyperextension.** You do not have one, and D5 now does its
job better.

**Removed · reverse cable / EZ curl, 2 sets.** This is the second of the "couple
of sets less". It is the last slot of the session, so it is the one you are
least fresh for, and forearms are among your best-served muscles at 14.9 sets
across six sessions — E and F both hit them again.

## What this does not fix

**Legs are still twice a week and that is deliberate**, as you asked. B is the
bilateral, heavier day; D is the unilateral, higher-rep, glute-and-hinge day.
They share machines and share almost no exercises.

**The soleus stays untrained.** The only fix is improvising a seated calf raise.

**F is still 62 minutes** — the real outlier, 25 to 44 minutes shorter than
anything else. I have deliberately left it alone: you asked for a D change, and
F is short on purpose ("a cheap bridge… light enough that a heavy A can follow
it the next day"). If you want it fuller, the cheapest move is the two slots
removed from D above — the biceps and a calf set do not touch bench recovery.
Say so and I will write that as a separate change.

**Two things I flagged earlier are still open and are not part of this**: the
triceps long head at 9.0 sets against 20.5 for the other two heads, and no
rotator-cuff or serratus work anywhere in the muscle model.
