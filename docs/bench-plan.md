## 2. The recalibration single — how to run it

**Go for 120 in the next session C.** You are right that a made lift is the
one thing that cannot be faked, and right that you need an anchor while your
RPE sense is still forming.

**Working up.** Do not take too many heavy singles before it — every one
costs you something on the attempt:

| | |
|---|---|
| 60 × 5 | |
| 80 × 3 | |
| 95 × 2 | |
| 105 × 1 | |
| 112.5 × 1 | last warm-up |
| **120 × 1** | the attempt, **paused, one second** |

**Paused.** If it only goes touch-and-go it is not the anchor you want — it
will not compare with anything, and the whole point is a number you can trust
later.

**Get a spotter.** Physically standing there. This is the one lift in the
plan where that is not optional.

**If you make it:** confirm it as your working max in the app (§3), and every
prescription in the plan recalibrates off it immediately.

**If you miss it:** that is also information and it costs you nothing — you
have a made 115 and a missed 120, which brackets your max within 5 kg. Take
117.5 the following week rather than 120 again.

### +2.5 kg a week

**Run it.** It is a reasonable experiment and it is the fastest way to find
your actual rate. Two things make it safe:

1. **Only ever add after a made lift.** A miss means repeat, not retreat.
2. **Log every attempt, made or missed.** A missed 122.5 is data. Right now
   you have no record of a miss anywhere, which means the app cannot tell the
   difference between "not attempted" and "attempted and failed".

Expect it to run for a few weeks and then stop. When it stops, that is the
signal to move to 1.25 kg a week or to every second week — not to push
harder.

## 3. The button you forgot

**Progress → Strength → Working maxes.** The app proposes a new max from your
**index sets** and you confirm it with a tick. Nothing is applied on its own —
that is deliberate.

Two rules it applies:

- **At a block boundary**, it proposes the best observed estimate from that
  block's index sets, and lists the sets it came from so you can see whether
  you believe them.
- **Mid-block, a max only rises**, and only when observed beat it by more than
  5% in **two consecutive rotations**. A bad week is a bad week, not a strength
  loss.

**Your export has no confirmed maxes at all** — every prescription you have
seen came from the seed of 115 and never moved. That is why the app kept
prescribing 97.5 kg back-offs while you were doing 105. Confirm the max after
the 120 and the whole plan recalibrates.

## 4. Percentage or RPE — the important question

> *"I am not sure should I use the weight based on what rpe should it be or
> what I feel like it is since my feeling is still not accurate."*

**Set the weight from the percentage. Log the RPE as information. Do not use
your feel to pick the weight yet.**

The reasoning is your own: your RPE sense is not calibrated. On 1 September
you logged **115 × 1 at RPE 9** and **110 × 1 at RPE 6** in the same session.
Those cannot both be true. If you pick loads from that, the load inherits the
error.

A percentage of a *made* 120 kg is an objective number. It cannot drift with
your mood, the gym crowd, or how the first rep happened to feel.

**So the loop is:**

1. Prescribe the variation at the **bottom** of its percentage range.
2. Do it. **Log the RPE afterwards** — do not let it change the weight that day.
3. After three or four exposures, look at what the RPEs actually were. If a
   variation has been consistently easier than intended, raise its percentage.
   If consistently harder, lower it.

**That is how you calibrate the percentages to yourself**, and it is the thing
you said you wanted to do later. It requires no accuracy from your RPE on any
single day — only that the average of several is informative, which it is.

### On "the same set can feel like RPE 7 and RPE 9"

That is normal and it has two causes, and only one is a problem.

**The one that is fine:** a genuinely different day. Sleep, food, the room,
whether you are annoyed. This is exactly what RPE exists to absorb.

**The one to fix:** inconsistent technique. If the bar path, the pause length
and the grip differ between two sets, they are not the same set and there is
nothing to compare. Your bounce dependence makes this worse — a rep with a
big bounce and a rep without are a long way apart in difficulty at the same
weight.

**Three things that make an RPE rating better:**

1. **Rate the bar speed, not the effort.** The question is not "how hard did
   that feel" — it is **"how many more could I have done at that speed?"** A
   set that hurt but moved fast is not an RPE 9.
2. **Rate it before you rack the bar**, not while catching your breath.
3. **Only rate the last set of a group.** Rating each of five identical sets
   gives you five guesses; rating the one that mattered gives you one answer.

## 5. Your sticking point, and what it means

> *"my sticking point is somewhere around my upper arm being horizontal and a
> bit above horizontal"*

That is the **transition zone** — roughly a third of the way up, and it is the
most common sticking point there is. It is where the press stops being a chest
movement and becomes a triceps movement.

**Off the chest**, the pecs are long and strong and the stretch reflex helps.
**At lockout**, the triceps have a good angle. **In between**, at exactly the
height you describe, the pecs have shortened past their best leverage and the
triceps have not yet reached theirs. Nobody is at their strongest there. You
stall there because that is where the handoff happens and yours is not smooth.

### Why this fits everything else in your data

You get **eight reps at 87%** touch-and-go, when a paused set at that load
should be four or five. The bounce is worth a great deal to you.

Put those two facts together and the picture is coherent: **the elastic energy
launches you off the chest, carries you into the transition zone, and runs out
exactly where your triceps have to take over — and they cannot.**

Which means the two problems are one problem. Fixing the handoff fixes both.

### And it makes an earlier finding urgent

Your triceps **long head gets 9.0 sets a week against 20.5 for the lateral and
medial heads.** The long head crosses the shoulder, so it is only loaded at
long length with the arm overhead — pushdowns and close-grip work barely touch
it. Every long-head set you get comes from A8 and E7.

I flagged that a week ago as a general observation. With a transition-zone
sticking point it is no longer general: **it is the muscle that has to take
over at exactly the height where you stall, and it is your least-trained
one.** A second overhead extension slot is now the change I would argue for
hardest on the pressing side.

## 6. The variation programme

### The percentages

Of your **paused competition max**. Once you have 120 confirmed, the kilos are
in the right column. **Start at the bottom of each range** and let three or
four exposures tell you where you actually sit.

| Variation | % of comp 1RM | At 120 kg | Trains |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Close grip** | **88–93%** | 105–112 | The triceps handoff — your sticking point |
| **Long pause, 3 s** | **85–92%** | 102–110 | Force from a dead stop; kills the bounce |
| **Pin press at sticking height** | **85–92%** | 102–110 | The stall itself, with zero elastic help |
| **Spoto (stop 2–3 cm up)** | **85–90%** | 102–108 | Tension and control, no contact |
| **Wide grip** | **95–100%** | 114–120 | Shorter range, harder lockout |
| **Feet-up / Larsen** | **90–95%** | 108–114 | Upper body without leg drive |
| Board press, 2-board | 100–108% | 120–130 | Above the sticking point only |
| *Touch-and-go* | *102–105%* | *122–126* | *Not a training tool. This is where the inflation came from* |

**This is the answer to "I used too much weight on variations."** You were
running them at close to your competition load. A close-grip bench at 115 is
not close-grip work, it is a maximal attempt with a bad grip. **At 105–110 it
is training.**

### Which, and when

Blocks are eight rotations. **One variation per block** — you cannot progress
something you meet once, and eight exposures is enough to add weight to it
three or four times.

| Block | Variation | Load | Why this one, for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Now** | **Close grip** | 105 → 112 | Directly trains the handoff you stall at. Easy to load, easy to progress, and it transfers to the competition lift more than anything else. |
| **Next** | **Pin press, bar set just below the stall** | 102 → 110 | The most specific attack there is: you start dead at the height where you stop. Once close grip has built the triceps, this teaches you to apply them there. |
| **Then** | **Long pause, 3 s** | 102 → 110 | By now the bounce should matter less anyway. This confirms it and builds the bottom. |
| **Later** | **Wide grip** or **feet-up** | 114–120 / 108–114 | Variety, and a second reading on the same lift. Neither addresses your weak point; they are for after it is addressed. |

**Why close grip before the long pause**, which is the opposite of what I said
last time: because your AMRAP is becoming a paused 100 kg every week. That
already gives you a weekly paused exposure at a meaningful load. The variation
slot should spend itself on the thing nothing else in the plan covers — the
triceps through the transition.

**How to set the pin press when you get there.** Set the pins so the bar
starts with your upper arm **just below horizontal** — about 2 cm under where
you stall. You want to press *through* the sticking point, not from above it.
If you can only set it above, it becomes a board press and trains the lockout
instead, which is not your problem.

### The one thing to add regardless of variation

**A second overhead triceps extension**, 3 × 10–15 at RPE 10. See §5. It is
three sets and it is aimed at exactly the muscle your sticking point needs.
