## What you have, confirmed

**Have:** leg press (loads light enough for one leg) · hip thrust **machine** ·
quad extension · seated leg curl · standing calf raise · **45° back extension** ·
**adductor / abductor machines** · cables · a bar to hang from.

**Do not have:** hack squat · lying leg curl · seated calf machine · reverse
hyperextension · standing or kneeling single-leg curl.

**Have but should not use:** Bulgarian split squat and Smith squat (your back) ·
Nordic curl (never performed).

**Five of D's twelve slots are not performable as written.** That is the whole
problem. Session B is five for five and nothing below touches it.

## Slot 1 · The quad slot
*Currently D2 · Hack squat or Bulgarian split squat · 3 × 8–12 @ RPE 8*

| | What it buys | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Single-leg leg press** *(3 × 10–15 per leg @ RPE 9)* | More range at the hip, nowhere for the strong side to hide, half the systemic cost per set. You measure 55.6 vs 56.2 cm — there is something to find. | Twice the time. Two sets per "set". |
| **B · Bilateral leg press, feet low and narrow** *(3 × 8–12 @ RPE 9)* | Simplest. Deeper and slower than B's version, so it is a different stimulus at the same machine. | Repeats B's exercise with a foot position change. The least interesting option. |
| **C · Quad extension, second dose** *(3 × 15–20 @ RPE 10)* | Trains the rectus femoris, which no press can. Cheap and quick. | Not a replacement for a press — it is an isolation exercise standing in for a compound. |

**My pick: A.** It is the only option that adds something you have none of, and
unilateral work is the honest answer to "I have no hack squat and squats hurt".

## Slot 2 · The hamstring slot
*Currently D3 · Lying leg curl or Nordic · 3 × 8–12 @ RPE 10*

The hamstrings do **two separate jobs** — bending the knee and extending the
hip. The plan only ever trained the first. This is the slot where that gets
fixed or does not.

| | What it buys | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A · 45° back extension** *(3 × 10–15 @ RPE 8)* | The hip-extension job, which is currently **zero sets a week**. Also loads the glutes at a long length, where the hip thrust loads them short. | Knee-flexion work stays at B's 4 sets a week. |
| **B · Second seated leg curl** *(3 × 12–20 @ RPE 10)* | Knee flexion up to 7 sets a week. Same machine as B, different rep range. | Hip extension stays at zero. Repeats B. |
| **C · Both** *(back extension 3 × 10–15, seated curl 2 × 12–20)* | Both jobs covered. Hamstrings properly trained for the first time. | Two extra sets, against your "a couple of sets less". |

**My pick: A**, with C as the upgrade if you decide length is not the problem.
For your back on the back extension: stop the top of the rep when your torso is
**in line** with your legs, never above it.

## Slot 3 · The second lower-body slot
*Currently D4 · Leg press · 2 × 12–20 @ RPE 9*

Not broken — you can do it — but it is the least useful slot in the session,
because D3 or D2 is already a leg press.

| | What it buys | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Hip thrust, machine** *(3 × 10–15 @ RPE 8)* | **Your glutes get 3.0 sets a week, in one session out of six** — by a distance the worst-served muscle in the plan against a 10–20 band. This takes it to 8.1. Machine, so it is quick to set up. | One more set than the slot it replaces. |
| **B · Leg press, feet high and wide** *(2 × 12–20 @ RPE 9)* | Glutes and adductors at the same machine you are already at. No setup at all. | Much weaker than a hip thrust for glutes. Third leg press of the week. |
| **C · Remove it** | Two sets back towards "a couple less". | Glutes stay at 3.0. |

**My pick: A**, and it is the single most valuable change on this page. If you
only take one thing from this document, take this one.

## Slot 4 · The core slot
*Currently D8 · Ab wheel rollout · 3 × 8–15 @ RPE 9*

You have never performed a rollout and are unsure of the form. It is also the
exercise most likely to teach a bad habit under load, with a back that already
objects.

| | What it buys | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Hanging leg raise** *(3 × 8–15 @ RPE 9)* | You have done leg raises. Trains the lower abs and hip flexors. Loads easily — reps, then a dumbbell between the feet. | Grip may fail before the abs do. Straps fix that. |
| **B · Pallof press** *(3 × 12–20 per side @ RPE 8)* | Anti-rotation, which exists **only at E10** — miss session E and you miss it for the rotation. | Does not train the abs the way a raise does. Different job. |
| **C · Keep the ab wheel, learn it** *(3 × 8–15 @ RPE 9)* | The best anti-extension exercise there is, if learned properly. Roll toward a wall and move the wall back over weeks. | The learning curve is where the risk is. |
| **D · Kneeling cable crunch, second dose** | Already at B7, so you know it. | Repeats B. |

**My pick: A.** If you want the anti-rotation gap closed too, A plus B is four
minutes.

## Slot 5 · The reverse hyperextension
*Currently D9 · Reverse hyperextension · 2 × 12–15 @ RPE 7*

Your gym does not have one.

| | What it buys | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Remove it** | Two sets back. If you took the back extension at slot 2, its job is already done — and done better, since the reverse hyper's swing is mostly momentum. | Nothing, if slot 2 is the back extension. |

**My pick: A** if you want the session shorter, **B** if you do not mind the two

## Slot 6 · The calf slot
*Currently D10 · Seated calf raise · 4 × 12–20 @ RPE 10*

You have no seated calf machine. **This is the only thing in your leg training
that is genuinely absent rather than substituted**: the soleus needs a bent
knee, and a standing raise cannot give it one.

| | What it buys | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Standing calf raise, 3 sets** *(3 × 10–15 @ RPE 10)* | Simple, available, and one set back. | **The soleus goes untrained** — both your calf sessions become gastrocnemius. |
| **B · Improvised seated calf raise** *(4 × 12–20 @ RPE 10)* | The only way to train the soleus, which is the larger of the two by cross-section. Bench, ball of foot on a plate, a heavy dumbbell across the knees just behind the kneecap with a pad. | Crude. You have to be willing to do it. |
| **C · Leg-press calf raise** *(3 × 12–20 @ RPE 10)* | No setup if you are already at the leg press. | Knee is straight, so it is the gastrocnemius again. Same gap as A. |
| **D · Remove calves from D** | Four sets back. Calves have the largest genetic spread and the smallest return, and this is a bench-focused bulk. | Calves down to 4 sets a week. |

**My pick: A**, on the grounds that you will actually do it. **B is better
training** and I will put it in if you say you would use it.

will not appear in your volume analytics. The work is real; the app cannot
see it. Adding the muscle is a separate change to the plan data.
